Simple Homemade Sushi

Japan

Cultural Background

Sushi originated in Southeast Asia as a method of preserving raw fish in fermented rice (narezushi). Over centuries in Japan, it evolved into Edo-style sushi (edomae sushi), where fresh raw fish was served over seasoned vinegared rice for quick consumption. Today, maki (rolled sushi) and nigiri are enjoyed globally as iconic representations of Japanese culinary art, emphasizing simple, fresh, and high-quality ingredients.


Did You Know?

Did you know that wrapping your bamboo rolling mat in plastic wrap prevents sushi rice from sticking to the mat, making both rolling and cleanup vastly easier?

Ingredients

Sushi Rice & Seasoning

  • 2 cups Short-Grain Sushi Rice
  • 2 cups Water
  • 3 tbsp Rice Vinegar
  • 1 tbsp Sugar
  • 1 tsp Salt

Fillings & Essentials

  • Nori (Dried Seaweed Sheets)
  • Sashimi-grade Salmon or Tuna (or cooked fillings like crab sticks)
  • Cucumber (julienned)
  • Avocado (thinly sliced)

To Serve

  • Soy Sauce
  • Wasabi
  • Pickled Ginger

How to Prepare

  1. Prepare Sushi Rice: Rinse sushi rice until the water runs clear. Cook in a rice cooker or stovetop with equal parts water. Combine rice vinegar, sugar, and salt; fold gently into warm cooked rice and let cool to room temperature.
  2. Setup Mat: Cover a bamboo sushi mat (makisu) with plastic wrap to prevent sticking. Place a sheet of nori shiny-side down on the mat.
  3. Spread Rice: Wet hands with water (to prevent sticking) and spread a thin layer of sushi rice evenly across the nori, leaving about 1 inch empty at the top edge.
  4. Add Fillings: Lay strips of fish, cucumber, and avocado horizontally across the lower third of the rice layer.
  5. Roll Sushi: Lift the edge of the mat closest to you and firmly roll over the fillings, tucking them in tightly as you roll forward until sealed.
  6. Slice & Serve: Use a sharp, wet knife to cut the roll cleanly into bite-sized pieces. Serve with soy sauce, wasabi, and pickled ginger.
